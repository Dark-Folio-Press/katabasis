De La Lune

REENA THE HARPY
A borrowed lullaby. The opening line lifts the 18th-century French nursery rhyme "Au clair de la lune" — a neighbor asking for a light to finish writing by — and turns the request for fire into a flirtation, then into cosmology: the same spark that lights a candle is the same fire every atom in us once burned in.

Au clair de la lune, ma chandelle est morte.

Tu as du feu ?

De la lune. De la lune. De la lune.

Nous naissons des cendres d'étoiles mortes.

By the light of the moon, my candle is dead.

Do you have a spark?

From the moon. From the moon. From the moon.

We are all born from the ashes of dead stars

Au clair de la lune, ma chandelle est mortes.

Tu as du feu ?

De la lune. De la lune. De la lune.

Nous naissons des cendres d'étoiles mortes.
